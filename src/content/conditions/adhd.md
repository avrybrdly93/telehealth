This page is educational and isn't a diagnosis. A psychiatric evaluation is how we understand your specific situation.

## What ADHD can feel like in adults

ADHD is often seen as a childhood condition. But it doesn't just go away at 18. For many adults, it continues in a different form.

Children with ADHD are often visibly hyperactive. Adults more often notice something else. Trouble staying focused on tasks that aren't interesting. Trouble organizing and finishing projects. Losing track of time. Misplacing things. A restless feeling of being "on the go." Acting or speaking before fully thinking it through.

Many adults with ADHD notice a pattern. They start things with a lot of energy. Then they struggle to follow through. It can feel like working harder than others just to keep up with everyday life.

Some adults were diagnosed as children. The pattern simply carried into adulthood. Others were never evaluated at all. Their symptoms may have looked different from the stereotype. Or they built workarounds that hid the difficulty for years. A change in life — a new job, more responsibility, less structure — can make the pattern harder to manage. Both paths are common. Neither means someone wasn't trying hard enough.

The National Institute of Mental Health estimates that about 4% of U.S. adults live with ADHD ([NIMH, ADHD in Adults](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/prevalence/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-among-adults.shtml)). For an adult diagnosis, occasional forgetfulness isn't enough on its own. Symptoms are usually longstanding. They show up in more than one part of life — work, home, relationships. And they cause real difficulty, not just occasional inconvenience.

## How psychiatric care typically helps

A psychiatric evaluation looks closely at your history. That includes, where relevant, how far back your symptoms go. It also looks at how attention, organization, and impulse control affect your life today. From there, a plan of care may include:

- **Medication management.** Medication is a well-established part of ADHD treatment for many adults. It can improve focus and impulse control. Whether it's a fit for you is worked out individually with your provider. This page doesn't recommend a specific medication.
- **Coordination with therapy or coaching.** Some adults pair medication management with therapy or ADHD coaching focused on daily-life strategies. Your provider can coordinate with an existing therapist, or help you think through whether that's useful.
- **Ongoing follow-up.** ADHD care, especially medication management, usually involves follow-up visits. These check how things are working and adjust the plan as needed.
